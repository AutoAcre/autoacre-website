The Technology

What lets autonomous mowing actually work on acreage

Most "robot mowers" are engineered for a quarter-acre suburban lawn. They use a buried boundary wire and dead reckoning. They drift. They miss strips. They can't handle a real slope. AutoAcre's systems are commercial-grade — built around two pieces of technology that change what's possible at acreage scale.

GPS-RTK precision

RTK is centimetre-accurate satellite positioning — about 1,000× more precise than the GPS in your phone. The mower knows exactly where it is on your property to within 2 cm. It mows the same paths every time, no missed strips, no drift, no buried boundary wire to cut or replace. Property mapped once, maintained forever.

LiDAR obstacle avoidance

LiDAR is the laser-based sensing system used in autonomous vehicles. The mower scans its surroundings in real time and stops or routes around obstacles dynamically — fallen branches, garden hoses, sleeping dogs, kids' bikes left in the grass. Acreage isn't a controlled environment; LiDAR is what makes autonomous operation safe in a real-world property.

Why both together

RTK without LiDAR is precision blind to anything new. LiDAR without RTK is reactive but loses position. Combined, you get a mower that knows where it is, where it should be, and what's in front of it — the dividing line between a robotic toy and an autonomous system that genuinely handles acreage. That's what every commercial-grade unit AutoAcre installs is built around.
